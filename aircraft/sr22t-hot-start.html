SR22T G7+ Hot Start Procedure

Aircraft: Cirrus SR22T G7+ (N269PG)
Engine: Continental TSIO-550-K (315 BHP)
Verified: 2026-07-10 | AFM + COPA Community

Overview

This is a verified hot-start procedure for the Cirrus SR22T G7+, synthesized from the G7 AFM (FAA-approved) and COPA forum consensus from experienced pilots. Definition: Hot start = engine shutdown less than 15 minutes.

Key Finding: Warm engine = NO priming required. The AFM explicitly states this. Use BOOST pump only (not HIGH BOOST).

The Procedure (Summary)

Pre-Start

  • Flight controls, instruments, fuel quantity verified
  • CAS messages cleared

Start Setup

  • Fuel Pump: BOOST (ON)
  • Mixture: FULL LEAN (or almost closed for very warm engine)
  • Power Lever: 1/4 to 3/4 forward (less is often better)
  • Engine Knob: BOTH
  • Brakes: HOLD

Crank & Start

  • Starter: ENGAGE
  • While Cranking: Slowly advance mixture toward RICH
  • Engine fires as mixture reaches operating range
  • When fires: Release starter, retard power to ~1000 RPM
  • Oil Pressure: Must rise within 30 seconds

After Start

  • Mixture: Lean until RPM peaks, leave it there
  • ALT 1 & 2 switches ON
  • Avionics: Initialize
  • CAPS pin: Remove & stow

Critical Points

1. Mixture Advance During Crank

This is the make-or-break step. Advance the mixture control slowly and deliberately while cranking. Do not advance it before cranking, and do not advance it too quickly. The engine should fire as the mixture reaches the operating range.

2. Power Lever (Throttle) Less is More

Start with 1/4 open. Many successful hot starts happen at this setting. If no fire after two attempts, try 3/4 open. If the engine fires and power is high, pull back immediately to prevent surge.

3. Oil Pressure is Your Go/No-Go

Engine must show rising oil pressure within 30 seconds of start. If no pressure rise after 60 seconds in cold weather, shut down and troubleshoot. This is not optional.

4. Starter Condition Matters

The stock Cirrus starter is weak. Many COPA pilots report upgrading to a Tempest aftermarket starter dramatically improves hot-start reliability and reduces cranking time. If you're struggling, this is worth discussing with your mechanic.

Flooded Engine Recovery

Signs of flooding: Weak intermittent firing, black smoke from exhaust.
  1. Fuel Pump → OFF
  2. Wait 5–10 seconds for fuel to drain
  3. Mixture Control → FULL LEAN
  4. Power Lever → FULL FORWARD
  5. Starter → CRANK 10–15 seconds
  6. When engine fires: Starter RELEASE, Power RETARD, Mixture SLOWLY advance
  7. Fuel Pump → ON
  8. Stabilize at ~1000 RPM

Troubleshooting

Symptom Check / Try
Won't crank Battery charge, fuel pump ON, mixture not CUTOFF
Sluggish crank Starter weak? Tempest upgrade available
Cranks but won't fire Engine Knob BOTH (not one mag), try fuel pump HIGH BOOST for 2 sec, try power 3/4
Rough after start Oil pressure rising? Mixture adjustment? Normal as engine warms

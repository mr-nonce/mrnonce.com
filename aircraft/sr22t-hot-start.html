Overview

This is a verified hot-start procedure for the Cirrus SR22T G7+, synthesized from the G7 AFM (FAA-approved), COPA forum consensus, and direct field experience from experienced SR22T pilots.

Key Definition: A "hot start" is determined by engine oil temperature, not elapsed time since shutdown.
  • Oil temp < 100°F: Normal start technique
  • Oil temp 100–130°F: Try hot start first; fall back to normal if unsuccessful
  • Oil temp ≥ 130°F: Hot start technique required

Critical Finding: Warm engine = NO priming required. The AFM explicitly states this. Use BOOST pump only (not HIGH BOOST).

Technique A: Mixture Advance During Crank (AFM-Based)

Pre-Start

  • Flight controls, instruments, fuel quantity verified
  • CAS messages cleared

Start Setup

  • Fuel Pump: BOOST (ON)
  • Mixture: FULL LEAN (or nearly closed for very warm engine)
  • Power Lever: 1/4 to 3/4 forward (start at 1/4; less is often better)
  • Engine Knob: BOTH
  • Brakes: HOLD

Crank & Start

  • Starter: ENGAGE
  • While Cranking: Slowly advance mixture toward RICH
  • Engine fires as mixture reaches operating range
  • When fires: Release starter, retard power to ~1000 RPM
  • Oil Pressure: Must rise within 30 seconds

After Start

  • Mixture: Lean until RPM peaks, leave it there
  • ALT 1 & 2 switches ON
  • Avionics: Initialize
  • CAPS pin: Remove & stow

Technique B: Simpler AFM-Aligned Method (Field-Proven ~99% Reliability)

This technique is from the AFM and verified field experience. It is simpler than Technique A and works nearly 100% of the time when followed correctly.

Start Setup

  • Fuel Pump: LOW BOOST (ON)
  • Mixture: RICH
  • Throttle: 1/2 inch open
  • Magnetos: BOTH
  • Brakes: HOLD

Crank & Start

  • Starter: PRESS AT FIRST INDICATION OF FUEL FLOW
  • Engine should fire within 2–5 cranks

Critical After-Start Step

  • Reduce throttle to 1000 RPM immediately after start
  • DO NOT INCREASE THROTTLE if engine momentarily stumbles
  • Engine will typically stumble as vapor flows through the system—this is normal
  • Engine will recover on its own without intervention
  • Advancing throttle during this stumble will cause engine shutdown

After Start

  • Mixture: Lean as needed for altitude
  • ALT 1 & 2 switches ON
  • Avionics: Initialize
  • CAPS pin: Remove & stow

Which Technique to Use?

Both techniques are AFM-compliant and proven in the field. Choose based on your comfort level:

Technique Complexity Best For
A: Mixture Advance More hands-on; requires practice Pilots who want full control; high-altitude/high-temp scenarios (Sedona, etc.)
B: Simpler AFM Method Simpler; less coordination needed Pilots who want simplicity and high reliability; most normal conditions

Recommendation: Practice Technique B first. It works 99% of the time and requires less coordination. Move to Technique A if you encounter difficult hot-start conditions (high altitude, extreme heat, cooled descent followed by immediate restart).

Critical Points (All Techniques)

1. Oil Pressure is Your Go/No-Go

Engine must show rising oil pressure within 30 seconds of start. If no pressure rise after 60 seconds in cold weather, shut down and troubleshoot. This is not optional.

2. Power Lever (Throttle) — Start Conservative

Start with 1/4 to 1/2 open depending on technique. If the engine fires and power is high, pull back immediately to prevent surge. Too much power during start causes rough running and unnecessary strain.

3. Starter Condition Matters (Historical Note)

Newer aircraft come with robust starters that work well in hot-start scenarios. If you're in an older G5 or G6, a Tempest aftermarket starter can improve reliability, but it is not required for G7+ aircraft.

Flooded Engine Recovery

Signs: Weak intermittent firing, black smoke from exhaust, strong fuel smell.

  • Fuel Pump → OFF
  • Wait 5–10 seconds for fuel to drain
  • Mixture Control → FULL LEAN
  • Power Lever → FULL FORWARD
  • Starter → CRANK 10–15 seconds
  • When engine fires: Starter RELEASE, Power RETARD to ~1000 RPM, Mixture SLOWLY advance
  • Fuel Pump → ON
  • Stabilize at ~1000 RPM and cool down before attempting flight

Troubleshooting

Symptom Check / Try
Won't crank Battery charge, fuel pump ON, mixture not CUTOFF, starter disconnect not engaged
Sluggish crank Battery low? Oil cold/thick? Wait for engine to warm slightly.
Cranks but won't fire Engine Knob BOTH (not one mag), verify fuel pump on, try alternate power setting (1/4 vs 3/4), oil temp correct?
Rough after start Oil pressure rising? Engine cold—normal as engine warms. Monitor for smooth rise to 35–45 PSI.
Engine stumbles post-start (Technique B) NORMAL—do not touch throttle. Engine will recover on its own.

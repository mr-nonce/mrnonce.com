SR22T G7+ Hot Start Procedure

Aircraft: Cirrus SR22T G7+ (N269PG)

Engine: Continental TSIO-550-K (315 BHP)

⚠️ DISCLAIMER: This procedure is for informational reference only. You must verify all steps with your aircraft's AFM and your CFI before using this procedure. Incorrect hot-start technique can damage the engine or prevent engine start. The owner has reviewed Technique B with their CFI; Technique A is a community technique and has not been independently CFI-verified for this aircraft. Always prioritize the AFM and your CFI's guidance.

Overview

This hot-start procedure for the Cirrus SR22T G7+ is based on the AFM start sequence adapted for high engine temperatures, supplemented by community techniques and CFI review.

Key Definition: A "hot start" is determined by engine oil temperature, not elapsed time since shutdown.
  • Oil temp < 100°F: Normal start technique
  • Oil temp 100–130°F: Technique B first; normal start if unsuccessful
  • Oil temp ≥ 130°F: Hot start technique required — Technique B first; if no start, Technique A. Do not attempt a normal full-rich start.

Critical Finding: Warm engine = NO priming required. The AFM explicitly states this. Use BOOST pump only (not HIGH BOOST/PRIME).

Technique B: AFM-Aligned Method (Primary)

This technique follows the AFM start sequence. Reliable when followed correctly.

Pre-Start

  • CAS Messages: CHECK
  • Strobe Lights: ON
  • Propeller Area: CLEAR
  • Flight controls verified
  • Instruments verified
  • Fuel quantity verified

Start Setup

  • Fuel Pump: BOOST (The SR22T fuel pump switch has two positions: BOOST and HIGH BOOST/PRIME. There is no LOW BOOST position. Do not use HIGH BOOST/PRIME for a hot start.)
  • Mixture: RICH
  • Power Lever: 1/2 inch open
  • Engine Knob: BOTH
  • Brakes: HOLD

Crank & Start

  • Starter: PRESS AT FIRST INDICATION OF FUEL FLOW (Limit cranking to 10-second intervals with a 20-second cooling period between cranks, per AFM caution)
  • Engine should fire within 2–5 cranks

Critical After-Start Step

  • Reduce power lever to 1000 RPM immediately after start
  • DO NOT INCREASE POWER LEVER if engine momentarily stumbles
  • Engine will typically stumble as vapor flows through the system—this is normal
  • Engine will recover on its own without intervention
  • Advancing power lever during this stumble will cause engine shutdown

After Start

  • Mixture: Lean as needed for altitude
  • ALT 1 & 2 switches ON
  • Avionics: Initialize
  • CAPS pin: Remove & stow

Technique A: Vapor Purge — Mixture Advance During Crank (COPA Community Technique)

This is a community-sourced technique. It is NOT an AFM procedure. Use when Technique B is unsuccessful in extreme hot-start conditions.

Pre-Start

  • CAS Messages: CHECK
  • Strobe Lights: ON
  • Propeller Area: CLEAR
  • Flight controls verified
  • Instruments verified
  • Fuel quantity verified

Start Setup

  • Fuel Pump: BOOST
  • Mixture: FULL LEAN (or nearly closed for very warm engine)
  • Power Lever: 1/4 to 3/4 forward (start at 1/4; less is often better)
  • Engine Knob: BOTH
  • Brakes: HOLD

Crank & Start

  • Starter: ENGAGE (Limit cranking to 10-second intervals with a 20-second cooling period between cranks, per AFM caution)
  • While Cranking: Slowly advance mixture toward RICH
  • Engine fires as mixture reaches operating range
  • When fires: Release starter, retard power lever to ~1000 RPM
  • Oil Pressure: Must rise within 30 seconds

After Start

  • Mixture: Lean until RPM peaks, leave it there
  • ALT 1 & 2 switches ON
  • Avionics: Initialize
  • CAPS pin: Remove & stow

Which Technique to Use?

Technique B follows the AFM start sequence. Technique A is a community technique for difficult hot starts; it is not in the AFM.

Technique Complexity Best For
B: AFM-Aligned (Primary) Simpler; less coordination needed Primary method. Most conditions, most pilots.
A: Vapor Purge (Fallback) More hands-on; requires practice Only if Technique B fails in extreme heat (high altitude + high ambient temp + prolonged ground ops)

Recommendation: Master Technique B. If you encounter a no-start after two attempts with Technique B, try Technique A.

Critical Points (All Techniques)

1. Oil Pressure is Your Go/No-Go

Engine must show rising oil pressure within 30 seconds of start. If no pressure rise after 60 seconds in cold weather, shut down and troubleshoot. This is not optional.

2. Power Lever — Start Conservative

Start with 1/4 to 1/2 open depending on technique. If the engine fires and power is high, pull back immediately to prevent surge. Too much power during start causes rough running and unnecessary strain.

3. Starter Condition (G7+ Note)

Newer aircraft come with robust starters that work well in hot-start scenarios. The G7+ starter is adequate for hot starts when AFM procedures are followed.

Flooded Engine Recovery

Signs: Weak intermittent firing, black smoke from exhaust, strong fuel smell.

  • Fuel Pump → OFF
  • Wait 5–10 seconds for fuel to drain
  • Mixture Control → FULL LEAN
  • Power Lever → FULL FORWARD
  • Starter → ENGAGE (Limit cranking to 10-second intervals with a 20-second cooling period between cranks, per AFM caution)
  • When engine fires: Starter RELEASE, Power Lever RETARD to ~1000 RPM, Mixture SLOWLY advance
  • Fuel Pump → ON
  • Stabilize at ~1000 RPM and cool down before attempting flight

Troubleshooting

Symptom Check / Try
Won't crank Battery charge, fuel pump ON, mixture not CUTOFF, starter disconnect not engaged
Sluggish crank Battery low? Oil cold/thick? Wait for engine to warm slightly.
Cranks but won't fire Engine Knob BOTH (not one mag), verify fuel pump on, try alternate power lever setting (1/4 vs 3/4), oil temp correct?
Rough after start Oil pressure rising? Engine cold—normal as engine warms. Monitor for smooth rise to 35–45 PSI.
Engine stumbles post-start (Technique B) NORMAL—do not touch power lever. Engine will recover on its own.
